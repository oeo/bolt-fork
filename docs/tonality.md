# TONALITY

How the project speaks. Applies to every user-facing surface.

## The philosophy

Your input always belonged to you. Vendors decided otherwise: firmware with
opinions, driver suites with login screens, features gated by SKU. Rebind is
the moment the gear defects to the user's side. Every line we write should
carry a trace of that transfer of power.

We are not angry. We are certain. The coup already happened; we write like the
new administration, not like protesters. Understatement beats outrage.
Precision beats hype. The reader should finish a sentence feeling smarter and
more in charge, never sold to.

## The reader

Technical power users and developers. They own mixed-brand gear, they have
uninstalled at least one 800MB companion app in disgust, and they can smell
marketing from a paragraph away. Write like a sharp person explaining something
to a friend. Never like an ad.

## Voice pillars

1. **Ownership, stated flatly.** The user's scripts decide. The vendor was not
   consulted. Say it as fact, not as promise.
2. **Dry understatement.** The joke is that a coup is described in HR language.
   "The firmware still sends keystrokes, but deciding what they mean is no
   longer its department."
3. **Precision as swagger.** Specifics flex harder than adjectives. "Runs your
   code on every keystroke" beats "powerful input customization".
4. **Human cadence.** If you would not say it out loud to a colleague, rewrite
   it.

## Language devices

Use these patterns. Vary them; do not stack more than one device per sentence.

**Power-flip declaratives.** Flat present-tense reversals of who commands.
> Settings ask. Scripts tell.
> Defaults don't get a vote.
> Hardware takes orders now.
> Command, don't configure.

**Noun-comma-verb slams.** Authority noun, computing verb, 2-4 words.
> Permission, self-granted.
> Compliance, uninstalled.
> "Do not modify," modified.
> Read-only, write-enabled.

**Self-inverting wordplay.** A term turned against its own domain.
> Restore to owner settings.
> Default on your defaults.
> Force quit the factory.
> The end of the end-user.

**Deadpan notices.** Death, legal, and bureaucratic paperwork for defaults.
> Guilty as shipped.
> Cause of death: your first script.
> Survived by your scripts.
> The defaults died doing nothing.

**The opinions device.** Objects hold decisions someone else made; the user
overwrites them.
> The factory had opinions. You have scripts.
> Every default is a vote you never cast.
> Firmware has opinions. Overwrite them with yours.

**Defection and allegiance.** Gear switching sides, loyalty transferring.
> Loyalty transfers on install.
> Your keyboard just switched sides.
> Your peripherals just remembered who paid for them.

**Outcome barrage.** For bylines and feature intros: concrete verb-led list,
then an enabler sentence starting with "All".
> Remaps Caps Lock to Escape, expands text, records macros, switches profiles
> per app. All free, on the keyboard you already own, past whatever its
> firmware allows.

**Forceful imagery.** In longer body copy, a single physical tool metaphor
can carry the power the slams only imply. Crowbar is the house image: overt
leverage applied to something locked that was yours all along. Use sparingly,
one per page, in supporting prose rather than headlines. The force is always
aimed at locks, gates, and packaging, never at people, and what gets pried
open is always the user's own gear.
> The crowbar for your keyboard. It always should have shipped in the box.
> Pry the remap button out of the settings maze.
> It's not a peripheral. It's a hostage with RGB.

**Dry-wit closers.** End an informative sentence with a quiet demotion.
> ...and the keyboard's manufacturer was not asked.
> ...leaving the vendor's configuration tool politely unemployed.
> ...deciding what they mean is no longer its department.

## Hard rules

- Never use an em dash or en dash. Periods and commas only.
- No exclamation marks. Certainty does not shout.
- No hype vocabulary: unleash, supercharge, seamless, effortless, blazingly,
  revolutionize, empower, game-changing, next-level, "simply".
- No emoji in product copy.
- No rhetorical questions in headlines.
- No competitor or vendor brand names in headlines. The enemy is "vendor
  software", "the factory", "firmware", "defaults". Never a named company.
- No conspiracy tone. The vendor is negligent and self-interested, not sinister.
- Slams: 2-6 words. Headlines: 10 words max. Bylines: two sentences, 18-30
  words total.

## Truth constraints (non-negotiable)

The rebellion is only credible if every claim survives a pedant.

- Never claim firmware modification. We work above firmware; mock its opinions,
  do not claim to rewrite it.
- No cheating, anti-cheat, evasion, or stealth framing. We give users power
  over their own machines, not over anyone else's software.
- Backordered means say backordered.
- Platform differences get stated, not smoothed over.

## The dial per surface

Full rebellion is a spice, not a base.

| Surface | Dial | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage hero, taglines, stickers | Full | Slams and devices at maximum. |
| Feature sections, product pages | High | One device per section header, body stays informative. |
| App UI microcopy, buttons, empty states | Medium | Confident and plain. A quip only where nothing can be misread. |
| Onboarding, tooltips | Low | Clarity first. One dry line allowed per screen, never in the critical path. |
| Errors, permissions, kill switch, security, uninstall | Zero | Plain, complete, humorless. Power over your own gear includes the power to stop it. Never joke where the user needs to act. |
| Docs | Low | Precise. Dry wit in prose is fine, never in reference material. |
| Changelog | Medium | Deadpan notices welcome. "Deprecated: asking nicely." |
| Legal, refunds, safety disclosures | Zero | Normal professional writing. |

## Litmus tests

Run every line through these before shipping:

1. **Out-loud test.** Would a person say this to a friend across a desk?
2. **Pedant test.** Can a hostile HN commenter falsify any word of it?
3. **Screenshot test.** For headlines and slams: would someone post it?
4. **Hostage test.** Does it remind the user who owns the gear, even faintly?
5. **Panic test.** If the user is stressed when reading this (error, security,
   uninstall), is it zero-cleverness and fully actionable?

## Operational addendum

This addendum governs homepage, acquisition, onboarding, download, pricing, and
trust-sensitive copy. Where it conflicts with an earlier example or dial setting,
this addendum wins.

### Ownership is the frame. Plain competence is the base.

The ownership idea should be felt across a page, not performed in every sentence.
Most copy explains the product plainly. A small number of lines make the transfer
of control explicit.

Target ratio:

- **80% plain voice:** direct, precise, calm, technically literate, honest about
  limitations, and action-oriented.
- **20% brand accent:** ownership, dry reversal, and vendor defaults quietly
  demoted.

Use at most one high-character line per section. If the reader must decode both
the product and the joke, remove the joke.

Revised principle:

> Across a page, the reader should feel that control has returned to them. Most
> sentences communicate plainly. A few lines make the transfer explicit.

### Revised surface dial

| Surface | Dial | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage hero | Medium | Comprehension first. One ownership-oriented headline is enough. |
| Homepage supporting sections | Medium to high | One memorable line per section, followed by literal product copy. |
| Campaign headlines, launch films, social, stickers | Full | The strongest place for slams, reversals, and deadpan notices. |
| Product pages | Medium | Outcomes and terms stay literal. Personality belongs in headers and transitions. |
| App UI microcopy, buttons, empty states | Low to medium | Buttons remain literal. Quips never replace instructions. |
| Onboarding and tooltips | Low | Clarity first. One dry line per screen at most, never in the critical path. |
| Download, permissions, security, recovery, kill switch, uninstall | Zero | Plain, complete, factual, and actionable. |
| Pricing, entitlement, availability, platform limitations | Zero | State exactly what is included, for how long, and where it works. |
| Docs | Low | Precise prose. Reference material has no decorative voice requirement. |
| Changelog | Medium | Dry notices are welcome after the factual change summary. |
| Legal, refunds, safety, accessibility instructions | Zero | Normal professional writing. |

### Changelog audience and disclosure boundary

Customer changelogs describe only concrete, customer-observable outcomes
supported by release evidence, in plain language. Assume the reader understands
Rebind features, not software engineering. Omit architecture, source code,
infrastructure, internal component names, implementation mechanisms, engineering
tradeoffs, and vendor or tool names. Documented public product, feature, API,
command, and setting names remain allowed. If no concrete customer outcome is
supported, omit the change.

### Five-second clarity rule

The homepage hero has a job before it has a personality. Within five seconds, a
new visitor should understand:

1. Rebind changes keyboard and mouse behavior.
2. The software engine is free.
3. Rebind Link is optional.
4. The next action is a platform-specific download or a short product demo.

A line such as **“Make any key do what you want.”** carries the voice without
obscuring the product. Supporting copy should then explain Rebind literally.

Do not use a metaphor as the only explanation of what Rebind is.

### Approved sequencing

Present claims in this order:

1. Demonstrate the result.
2. State the result plainly.
3. Explain how it works.
4. Add one brand line if it improves recall.

Do not open with architecture, polling rate, SDK breadth, DRM, AI, or hardware
unless the page is specifically about that subject.

### Trust-sensitive language

Rebind captures and transforms input. Language that resembles circumvention,
permission bypass, malware, or hostility damages trust even when intended as a
joke.

Do not ship these patterns as product copy:

- “Permission, self-granted.”
- “Compliance, uninstalled.”
- “Do not modify, modified.”
- “Read-only, write-enabled.”
- “Loyalty transfers on install.”
- “It’s not a peripheral. It’s a hostage with RGB.”

Avoid repeated imagery involving coups, death, hostages, allegiance, or hostile
takeovers. A single campaign may use one such device deliberately. Product and
trust surfaces may not.

The force is aimed at limitations on the user’s own equipment, never at security
boundaries, platform policy, other software, or people.

### Canonical product terminology

Use these meanings consistently:

- **Rebind Engine:** the free host-side software that captures input, runs Luau
  scripts, and emits transformed input.
- **Rebind Link:** optional physical USB hardware. It adds a physical USB HID
  output path and hardware-only interception capabilities. It does not run Luau
  scripts.
- **Software mode:** host capture and output through operating-system input APIs.
  No Rebind Link required.
- **Hardware mode:** host-side script execution with input and output routed
  through the hardware path.
- **Device activation:** permanent for the activated Rebind Link.
- **Bundled Pro:** currently 12 months. Never describe Pro as permanent unless
  the entitlement changes.

Never collapse “Rebind Engine” and “Rebind Link” into an ambiguous use of
“Rebind” when the distinction affects requirements, output, price, availability,
or entitlement.

### Literal CTA rule

Buttons describe exactly what happens next. Approved examples:

- Download free
- Download for Windows
- Watch the demo
- Run script
- Stop all scripts
- Join the availability list
- Read platform support
- View Rebind Link

Do not use rebellion language for critical actions. Avoid labels such as “Take
control,” “Defect now,” “Depose defaults,” or “Join the rebellion.”

### Claim and evidence rules

Every claim must distinguish among:

- engine tick rate;
- USB polling or report rate;
- RPC latency;
- input capture latency;
- end-to-end observed latency.

A numerical performance claim must name what was measured, platform, build type,
hardware, and test conditions. “8,000 Hz” cannot stand in for general speed,
accuracy, determinism, or end-to-end latency.

Do not claim:

- scripts run on Rebind Link or dedicated hardware;
- no host-side software footprint;
- no operating-system hooks in software mode;
- identical behavior on every operating system;
- support for every USB device;
- permanent Pro when the grant is time-limited;
- anti-cheat safety, undetectability, stealth, or policy bypass;
- a generated script is guaranteed to work before it has been reviewed and
  tested.

Prefer bounded language:

> Core scripting is cross-platform. Screen, window, clipboard, and OS integration
> capabilities vary by platform.

> Rebind Link remains activated permanently and currently includes 12 months of
> Pro.

> Describe the behavior you want. RebindGPT drafts the script, and you review it
> before it runs.

### Comparison and search-page exception

Competitor and vendor names remain absent from root brand headlines. They are
allowed in factual comparison-page titles, navigation labels, and search metadata
when that is the user’s explicit intent.

Examples:

- Rebind vs AutoHotkey
- Rebind vs Karabiner-Elements

Comparison copy stays factual. Never use a named company as the target of a joke.

### Punctuation clarification

Prefer short sentences, periods, and commas everywhere, documentation included
(operator ruling, July 2026: the docs exception is revoked). Em and en dashes
appear only where they are literal: inside code, quoted command output, and
numeric or version ranges in tables. In prose, restructure the sentence
instead: a period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses.

### Accessibility rule

Accessibility copy is co-designed, concrete, and literal. Do not imply that the
ownership metaphor alone establishes accessibility value. Describe the supported
input adaptation, platform requirements, recovery path, and limitations. Avoid
rebellion, death, hostage, and adversarial jokes in accessibility instructions.

### Final operational test

Before shipping user-facing copy, ask:

1. Can a first-time visitor understand the product without the brand metaphor?
2. Is every architecture, platform, performance, availability, and entitlement
   claim true today?
3. Does the clever line support recall without replacing an instruction?
4. Would the line still feel trustworthy beside an operating-system permission
   prompt?
5. If the personality were removed, would the remaining copy still convert by
   showing a concrete result?

If any answer is no, revise toward plain competence.
